# Regret

### *An Adaptive Voyage Through Uncertain Routes*

> *When every road be hidden in uncertainty,
> and every triumph known but after the deed,
> what wisdom hath the traveller, save to choose
> and learn from whence his fortunes came?*

In a realm where no carrier may be trusted with certainty, the traveller must choose his road.

For every message that departeth the kingdom, one carrier must bear it forth. Yet the true worth of each carrier remaineth concealed. Their prices differ, their fortunes shift, and that which prospered at noon may falter ere nightfall. 

The messenger departeth.

The answer doth not.

A receipt of delivery may return anon, or after many minutes, or perhaps never. Thus each choice must be made whilst the knowledge of former choices remaineth incomplete. 

And so **Regret** must learn whilst it serveth.

It must discover which roads bear the safest passage, yet must not abandon the roads it deemeth poor. For fortune is fickle, and the fallen may rise whilst the favoured may falter. Every message entrusted unto a failing carrier, however, hath its price.

Thus must the learner balance **knowledge against action, certainty against exploration, and cost against delivery**. 

## The Trials

Many trials await the traveller:

* **Delayed tidings**
  Receipts may arrive late, out of order, or not at all.

* **Fickle fortune**
  A carrier's success may wax and wane without warning.

* **The price of passage**
  The surest road may not be the wisest, if its toll be too great.

* **The peril of certainty**
  Favour one road above all others, and thou shalt cease to learn when fortune turneth against it.

* **Many minds, one kingdom**
  Many replicas must share what they have learned, yet not so freely that all thought be made one and exploration be lost. 

## The Great Experiment

Yet before the service be trusted with the traffic of the realm, a **simulator** shall first be wrought.

Therein shall the truth be known.

The true fortunes of every carrier, their success, their cost, their delays, their failures, and the changing nature thereof, shall be known unto the simulator, whilst remaining hidden from the learner.

And by the use of a fixed seed, the very same sequence of events may be summoned again, that one strategy may be judged against another upon equal ground. 

Four doctrines shall enter the contest:

* **The Naive Baseline**
* **Epsilon-Greedy**
* **The Doctrine of Confidence Bounds**
* **The Way of Posterior Sampling** 

Their worth shall not be proclaimed by crown nor decree.

**It shall be measured.**

Their regret shall be observed. Their recovery from calamity shall be timed. The cost of each delivered message shall be reckoned. Their wisdom under delayed tidings shall be tested, and their conduct observed as the number of servants within the kingdom waxeth and waneth. 

## Of Many Machines

And herein lieth a subtler trial.

When many replicas labour together, each beholdeth but a portion of the kingdom's traffic. If they share their knowledge too freely, they may all come to favour the same road, and thus cease to explore.

If they share too little, each shall pay anew for wisdom already purchased by another. 

Thus there must exist a middle path.

Not silence.

Not perfect accord.

But **measured communion**.

And it shall be the task of Regret to discover how often knowledge ought to pass from one mind unto another.

## The Question

> *How oft must wisdom be shared amongst many minds,
> ere counsel cease to be counsel, and become but one voice?*

That is the question Regret seeketh to answer.

Not merely **which carrier is best**, but whether a kingdom of many learners may discover the answer together, whilst the world beneath them shifteth, the tidings arrive late, and yesterday's wisdom may become tomorrow's folly.

And should fortune favour the endeavour, the journey may yet continue toward routes chosen by the nature of each message, toward adversaries whose behaviour changeth with the traffic they receive, and toward the search for the best road when the roads themselves may vanish.
